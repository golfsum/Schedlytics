The gap native TikTok stats leave open

TikTok is built for reach. A single video can rack up hundreds of thousands of views overnight, and the native analytics will happily show you views, watch time, average view duration, and where your traffic inside the app came from. What they will not show you is what happens after someone taps away. Did that viral cooking clip actually send buyers to your recipe ebook? Did the product demo move anyone toward your store? Native stats go quiet at the exact moment that matters for your business.

There is a second wrinkle that is unique to TikTok. Unlike platforms where you can drop a link into a description or a pinned comment, TikTok funnels almost all of its outbound traffic through one spot: the single link in your bio. That makes a huge view count easy to celebrate and hard to act on, because you cannot tell which of your recent videos is the one actually driving people to tap that link.

Schedlytics closes both gaps. We want to be straight with you about how it works today. A direct TikTok account connection is rolling out and is awaiting platform approval, so native TikTok stats inside Schedlytics are coming soon. What you can do right now, with no approval and no waiting, is track every click your TikToks send by putting a Schedlytics tracked link in your bio.

Start with a tracked bio link

The fix starts with the one place TikTok lets you send people: your bio. Instead of pasting your raw homepage URL there, you use a Schedlytics tracked link. It looks like a clean, short, branded link to your visitors, but behind the scenes every tap is recorded with the source and campaign it belongs to. The moment someone moves from a video to your bio to your site, that journey is captured. If you want your links to carry clean campaign labels, the free UTM builder stamps them for you in seconds.

Because everything routes through that one link, your bio becomes the single most valuable piece of measurement real estate you own on TikTok. Treat it that way and you turn an anonymous flood of views into a record of who actually came to visit.

Rotate the bio link to attribute per video

One bio link sounds like a measurement dead end, but it is actually a lever. The trick is to rotate it. Before you publish a video you expect to perform, swap in a fresh tracked link tagged to that video or campaign. Every tap during that window is attributed to it. When the next push goes out, rotate again. Over a few weeks you build a clear picture of which videos sent traffic and which only sent applause.

If you do not want to swap the link constantly, point your bio at a tracked landing page that branches to your store, your newsletter, and your latest drop. You still get one bio link, but you learn which destination people actually wanted. For the full playbook on structuring that page, see our notes on link in bio strategy.

Measure which videos actually drive traffic

This is where the view count finally stops lying to you. A video with 800,000 views and 90 bio taps is a very different asset from a video with 40,000 views and 1,200 taps, and only click data can tell them apart. Schedlytics lines up reach against clicks so the quiet video that converts gets the credit, and the viral video that went nowhere gets a second look.

Once you can see traffic per video, your content calendar starts to plan itself. You make more of what moves people off the app and less of what only chases the feed. To compare TikTok against everywhere else you post, your full picture lives in social media analytics.

Connect clicks to revenue

Clicks are the bridge, not the destination. Because each tap is tied to a video and a campaign, you can follow that same thread all the way to a signup or a sale. Now you are not guessing whether TikTok is worth the effort. You can point at a specific video and say it drove this much traffic and this many sales, which is the number that actually settles the argument about where your time should go. And as the native connection comes online, those revenue stories will sit right next to your view and watch time data in one place.
