Pinterest is a traffic engine, not a social feed

It is easy to file Pinterest next to Instagram and TikTok and treat it like another social feed. That framing misses the point. Pinterest behaves far more like a visual search and discovery engine. People arrive with intent, search for an idea, and tap a pin to go somewhere. Almost every pin carries a destination URL, which means the whole platform is built around sending people off Pinterest and onto a website. That is exactly the kind of traffic you want to measure.

The catch is that native Pinterest stats stop at the edge of Pinterest. You can see impressions, saves, and outbound clicks inside the platform, but once a visitor lands on your page the trail goes cold. You cannot tell which pin sent the buyer, which board does the heavy lifting, or which idea quietly converts month after month. Schedlytics picks up the trail right where Pinterest drops it.

A tracked link as the pin destination

The mechanism is simple. Instead of pointing a pin straight at a raw URL, you set a Schedlytics tracked link as the destination. Now every tap is recorded with the pin and campaign it came from. You can read it as plain numbers, lining up "9,300 impressions" next to "410 clicks" and "22 signups," and finally see which pins earn their place. This is the same link tracking that powers the rest of Schedlytics, so your Pinterest traffic sits in the same dashboard as every other channel. If you would rather hand-build the link, the UTM builder creates a clean, tagged destination you can drop straight into a pin.

Pins are evergreen, so attribution over time matters

A tweet has a half-life measured in hours. A pin has a half-life measured in months. A good pin keeps resurfacing in search and related results long after you published it, and it keeps sending clicks the whole time. That changes how you should measure it. A first week report tells you almost nothing about a channel whose payoff is a long, slow tail of traffic. Schedlytics records every click for the life of the link, so you watch a pin from last spring quietly add visitors all year, and you give your back catalog the credit it earns. Which boards and pins actually send visitors

Not all of your pins pull their weight, and the ones that look best are not always the ones that drive traffic. A pin can rack up saves and still send very few people anywhere, while a plainer pin on the right board sends a steady stream of buyers. Once each pin has its own tracked link, you can group the links for a board or a theme into a campaign and compare them honestly. Suddenly it is obvious which boards deserve fresh pins and which ideas to repurpose. That grouping is what campaign tracking is built for.

Connecting clicks to revenue

Clicks are the start, not the finish. The reason to know which pin sent a visitor is so you can connect that visitor to a signup, a sale, or a booking. Because every Schedlytics click carries its source and campaign, you can trace revenue back to the specific pin and board behind it. That turns Pinterest from a place you hope is working into a channel you can measure, and it tells you exactly which idea to pin next.

Frequently asked questions

Does Schedlytics replace Pinterest Analytics?

No. Pinterest Analytics shows impressions, saves, and pin clicks inside Pinterest. Schedlytics adds what happens after the click: the visitors a pin actually sends to your site and the campaign each one belongs to.

How does it track clicks from a pin?

You set a Schedlytics tracked link as the destination URL of your pin. Every time someone taps through, the click is recorded with its source and campaign, so traffic ties back to that exact pin.

Why does attribution over time matter for Pinterest?

Pins are evergreen. A pin you published months ago can keep surfacing in search and sending clicks. Schedlytics records every click for the life of the link, so you see the long tail of traffic, not just the first week.

Can I see which boards drive the most traffic?

Yes. Group the tracked links for pins on a board into a campaign and Schedlytics rolls up the clicks, so you can compare boards and double down on the ones that send real visitors.
