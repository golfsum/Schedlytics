How to Find Your Best Performing Platform in a Week

You are posting everywhere and you still cannot say which platform earns its keep. Here is a simple seven day experiment that answers the question with clicks to your own site, not likes you cannot bank.

Why comparing platforms is so hard

Every platform hands you a dashboard, and every dashboard tells a different story. One counts a view after three seconds, another after a full second, another counts a thumbnail scrolling past. Impressions, reach, plays, engagements, saves. None of these numbers use the same definition, so stacking them next to each other tells you almost nothing.

Worse, none of those metrics answer the only question that pays your bills: how many people left the platform and came to your site. A post can rack up thousands of views and send nobody to your shop, your newsletter, or your booking page. The native tools stop at the edge of the app. To compare platforms fairly you need one metric that means the same thing everywhere, and the cleanest one is a click on a link you own.

The one week experiment, step by step

The setup is deliberately small. You are running a fair race, so every platform gets the same starting line and the same finish line. Here is how it works.

  • Pick one destination that matters, like a product page, a free guide, or your email signup.
  • Create one tracked link per platform, each pointing at that same destination. A link shortener that records clicks does this in seconds. See how link tracking works if you have not set one up before.
  • Label each link clearly so you can tell them apart later. Tagging the source, medium, and campaign with a UTM builder keeps your reports tidy and consistent.
  • Share each link only on its own platform for seven straight days. The Instagram link goes only in Instagram. The YouTube link goes only in YouTube descriptions. No cross posting the same link, or you lose the ability to tell them apart.
  • Keep your posting rhythm normal. Do not suddenly triple your effort on one platform, or you are testing your effort, not the platform.

That is the whole experiment. Same destination, separate links, one week, no cheating. When the week ends, every click is already sorted by the platform that produced it.

What to measure

Two numbers carry most of the weight. The first is total clicks, which tells you how much traffic each platform actually drove. The second is unique visitors, which strips out repeat taps and bots so you see how many real people showed up. When clicks look huge but unique visitors are modest, you are looking at a few enthusiastic fans, not broad reach.

The third number is the one most people skip, and it is the fairest of all. Take the clicks each platform sent and divide them by the reach that platform reported for the week. That is your click rate, and it puts a tiny audience and a giant audience on the same footing. A small platform that converts five percent of its viewers into visitors is often worth more per post than a large one that converts a fraction of a percent. Your social media analytics dashboard can line these up side by side so you are not stitching screenshots together by hand.

How to read the results without fooling yourself

Resist the urge to crown a winner from the weekly total alone. Open the daily breakdown first. A single post that catches fire can carry an entire platform to the top of the chart while the other six days were quiet. That is a lucky moment, not a reliable channel. If you see one giant spike and six flat days, mark it as an outlier and pay more attention to the steady performers underneath.

Watch the shape of the traffic too. Some platforms deliver a burst on the day you post and then go silent. Others, like search driven or evergreen feeds, keep sending visitors for days afterward. A platform with a smaller peak but a long tail can quietly out earn a flashier one over a full month. Grouping your week into a campaign makes that pattern easy to see, because the report sums the slow drip alongside the opening burst.

Finally, be honest about sample size. One week is enough to spot a clear leader, but a photo finish between two platforms is not a verdict. If two channels land within a few clicks of each other, run a second week before you commit. Cheap data beats a confident guess.

How to act on what you learn

Once a winner separates from the pack, the move is simple. Double down on the platform that sent the most real visitors per unit of effort. Post more often there, study which formats earned the clicks, and reuse what worked. This is where your time turns into traffic, so give it the bulk of your attention.

Do not abandon the others. Drop them to maintenance instead. Keep a light, consistent presence so you stay visible and keep collecting data, but stop pouring hours into channels that did not pull their weight. Re run the experiment once a quarter, because audiences shift and a platform that lagged in spring can surge by autumn. The point is not to find one answer forever. It is to keep a cheap, honest measurement running so your effort always follows the traffic instead of the hype.

Frequently asked questions

How long should the experiment run?

Seven full days is the sweet spot. It covers a complete week of audience behavior, including weekdays and the weekend, without giving any single post enough time to skew the totals. If your posting cadence is slow, stretch it to ten days so every platform gets a fair number of posts.

What if one platform has a much bigger audience?

Raw clicks favor the bigger audience, so look at click rate too. Divide clicks by the reach each platform reports for the week. A small platform that turns a high share of viewers into visitors can be more valuable per post than a large one that barely converts.

Should I look at clicks or unique visitors?

Look at both. Clicks tell you how much action a platform drove. Unique visitors tell you how many real people it sent, with repeat taps and bots stripped out. If clicks and unique visitors disagree sharply, trust unique visitors as the cleaner measure of audience.

What if a single post goes viral during the test?

One viral post can hide the true pattern. Look at the daily breakdown, not just the weekly total. If one platform won because of a single spike, note it as an outlier and weigh the steady day to day performance more heavily before you decide where to invest.
